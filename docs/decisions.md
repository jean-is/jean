# Decisions

Each entry is a decision that still binds: what was chosen, why, and what was rejected, so it isn't proposed again. An entry earns its place only if someone could undo it by mistake, or would propose again what it rejected. How something works goes into [architecture.md](architecture.md), [product.md](product.md) or [design.md](design.md), once, and the entry links there; a screen's layout, a control, a bug's cause, a measurement and a budget raise are no decisions, and git keeps them.

An entry is 200 words at most, its heading and its Rejected list included, the numbers heading it apart, and `test/decisions.test.js` checks that every number cited heads exactly one entry. When a later decision amends an entry, rewrite that entry to the new state and add the number to its heading. When an entry no longer binds, delete it and the citations of it. A number is never used again: a commit may cite one that is gone, and `git log -S'D123' -- docs/decisions.md` finds its last wording. A new decision takes the next number after the highest here, and after D666 whatever is here, checked again just before writing, since several sessions may add numbers at once.

## Product

- **D1 · Reboot from scratch** rather than refactor DEVS. *Why:* no users or years of fixes to protect; cutting 95% is harder than writing ~5k clean lines. *Rejected:* pruning DEVS ([background.md](background.md)).
- **D2 · D395 · D566 · Jean's promise is “Create with any AI, keep everything”: everyone's AI, non-technical people first, at home, in a family and at work, one box, anything in, anything out, with any brain.** What sets it apart, in this order: simple, universal, confidential, ready for a family, open, shareable, each shown by what it makes (D5; [product.md](product.md#who-and-what)). The promise names what ChatGPT can't: any brain, its rivals' included, and history, files and memory kept on the device, brought in and taken anywhere; “in a browser tab” says how. Files in, files out, searched most, leads the examples. *Why:* a files-first pitch hid most of Jean (17 October 2026); “Everyone's AI, in a browser tab” was as true of ChatGPT, while searches in five languages ask how to leave ChatGPT, keep its history and find every AI in one place (24 October). *Rejected:* files in, things out as the identity; developers first; generic chat; “private” (Proton and DuckDuckGo own it); a mode for code; “Everyone's AI, in a browser tab”; “AI operating system” and “AI workloads” (builders' words); “own your creations” (OpenAI's terms give users their output; it reads as copyright); “all AI” (aggregators); a full stop mid-promise (the title stops there).
- **D668 · The homepage's story is taking your ChatGPT history with you; ChatGPT is named there, never in the promise.** Any AI and the browser tab lead together, since neither is unique alone. *Why:* searches in five languages ask how to move that history and fear losing it. *Rejected:* “cancel and keep your history” (guides say ChatGPT keeps it after cancelling, so it promises what people already have); “quit ChatGPT” (reads as a political movement); “agent” as the brand (searches complete it with learning and building).
- **D473 · Jean keeps what it makes, tables first, then documents and decks (D485, D491).** A table Jean builds from a request and keeps up to date comes first ("keep track of my job applications": typed columns, a board by status, a routine); then documents kept and edited beside the conversation, Jean's changes accepted or rejected (D505); decks kept as files of the project, and .pptx out (D591). Each is born in a task and found as a capability is ([roadmap.md](roadmap.md)). *Why:* a database is Notion's strongest feature and its hardest to learn, and a brain takes that away; boards, calendars, forms and apps' data are views of a table; documents are expected everywhere, and decks are PowerPoint's and Gamma's. *Rejected:* every editor in Jean's own code (thousands of lines each); an office suite inside (Univer, OnlyOffice, BlockSuite: megabytes, a build, formats of their own); others or brains building the three (Japanese input, Arabic and screen readers left to chance, a format each); a tree of pages to organise (the one box, D5); decks edited as PowerPoint does.
- **D5 · Progressive discovery, like a game:** Jean starts as one box, and a capability's code loads when it is discovered. Search is there from the start, as the second way around the app besides the drawer that RGAA 12.1 asks for. *Why:* the simple first screen and the small first load are one decision. *Rejected:* a feature-complete UI from day one; search after ten conversations (the drawer was the only way around until then).
- **D433 · A capability found turns off, and on again, from its page in Settings › Capabilities** ([product.md](product.md#progressive-discovery), [architecture.md](architecture.md#stack)). *Why:* something found by chance, or no longer wanted, shouldn't stay for good. *Rejected:* unloading in place (its timers, listeners and elements go on: routines would still run); forgetting it was found (offered again); a switch in each capability's own section (thirty places); a second way for private windows (two behaviours); a note to conversations that declared its tools (they are gone, as a removed connection's are).
- **D6 · Discovery is triggered by events, reflexes and the brain's `offer()`, never by keywords.** *Why:* it works in every language. *Rejected:* regex or keyword intent rules.
- **D7 · D224 · D225 · One agent loop, plus helpers, meetings of the projects' assistants, and methods as ready-made skills.** Delegation is one tool (D98); a meeting has the other projects' assistants speak, without tools, and asks first, a project's memory being its own ([architecture.md](architecture.md#meetings)); six skills each pick a method ([product.md](product.md#instructions-and-memory)). *Why:* 2026's winners are single-loop agents; a project with a name is what others call an agent, so people ask their agents to meet; a method is steps, as a skill is. *Rejected:* DEVS's decomposer, standing teams with roles, persona libraries and agent patterns (greeter, operator, orchestrator, listener): the swarm; methodologies as modes of the loop; speakers with tools (acting stays the chair's); meetings among assistants, or endless rounds; a transcript per meeting; each speaker on its own project's brain (one answer mixing keys); a skill per method (a `<skills>` line each, tiles alike); a method field in `project.md` (instructions say it); developers' methods (test-, behaviour- and domain-driven design, XP, the V-model, RAD, Crystal, SAFe, Scrum for software), the first user writing no code (D2); TQM (a culture); Six Sigma beyond DMAIC; YOLO (Jean without a skill).
- **D27 · D242 · Data is bound to the origin, so the domain is settled before launch: jean.is, bought.** The desktop app's bundle id is its reverse, `is.jean.desktop`. *Why:* changing it means moving everyone's data. *Rejected:* launching on a temporary domain; jean.cx.
- **D38 · D50 · Jean is its own project, in its own repository,** moved out of DEVS with its history (`git subtree split`): nothing read, imported or copied from outside the repository, and no devs.new redirect. *Rejected:* Jean as DEVS's successor; a DEVS subfolder.
- **D3 · D39 · D139 · Open core: MIT, with the enterprise integrations in `ee/` under the Elastic License 2.0 and a key.** Reputation first, money second. Everything a person or a team uses is MIT; `ee/` holds only what an organisation's IT, security or compliance team asks for, never the person's own security, a standard Jean speaks or anything already open, and nothing moves from open to `ee/`. One module loads `ee/` with `import()`, only when policy holds a key, checked offline inside `ee/`; `ee/GRANT.md` makes every version MIT if no key can be bought for twelve consecutive months ([enterprise.md](enterprise.md#open-and-enterprise)). *Why:* small players get the whole product, big ones adopt it bottom-up and pay when their IT needs the integrations; Elastic-2.0 is short, on the SPDX list and known to legal teams; Ed25519 would need browsers above D40's floor. *Rejected:* BUSL on everything; AGPL with a commercial licence; FCL (not on the SPDX list); PolyForm Small Business; a custom licence; online key checks; keys that stop a running version; locked features shown in the open version.
- **D45 · D226 · D477 · One relay for what needs browsers to reach each other: a person's devices (D227, D700), the people they send to or work with, and later requests between people's Jeans.** Its mailboxes are named by the hash of the token that opens each, as hst.ing's shares are (D361), and hold for 7 days messages sealed between devices' or people's keys; it holds no key or name, and nothing about who reads. Its journals hold, in order, the sealed changes of what people work on together (D733). Jean's maintainer runs one on a subdomain of Jean's domain, which jean.is's policy names (`urls.relay`); an organisation runs its own, or none. Replies to a shared document or table come sealed to the sender to merge (D477). A colleague's request arrives as a card, answered by the recipient's own brain and rules (D101), approved before anything goes back. *Why:* a person's Jean lives on their devices; reaching it from another device or person needs a meeting point, not a server that reads. *Rejected:* a relay that can read content, or holds keys it could swap; hst.ing, whose shares are for others; a Jean server that runs agents; a request run with the sender's authority.
- **D736 · A closed Jean is nudged when something comes for its person: the relay asks each device's browser push service to wake it, and the nudge holds nothing** ([architecture.md](architecture.md#the-relay)). Where the browser may notify (Notifications on) and the relay's configuration has a VAPID key, each device keeps its push subscription beside the person mailbox; a message there sends a Web Push with no payload, signed by the relay, once in ten minutes at most and never while a device reads the mailbox, and the service worker says only that something came, in words the page left. Endpoints only at the push services the configuration names; Notifications turned off takes the device off. *Why:* what People and Together send waits unseen for 7 days while Jean is closed; with no payload, a push service learns only a time. *Rejected:* a payload sealed to the device (a lock screen would show who sent what); mail or texts from the relay (it knows no address); posting to any endpoint (a forwarder for anyone); a nudge per message (noise, and timing for the push service).

## Stack and budgets

- **D9 · D43 · D51 · D52 · D74 · D243 · D247 · D318 · No build step and nothing generated or third-party committed; a deploy serves one folder, `app/`, which the web's copy minifies and compresses** ([architecture.md](architecture.md#stack)). *Why:* the code that runs is the code written; the web's copy loads 40% less; third-party code in history is noise; one folder to copy. *Rejected:* a Vite or TypeScript build; a bundle for the web (11 KB less; known paths renamed, the shell resent); source maps; minifying third-party code (mostly minified; but ProseMirror's bundle, D485); esbuild's rewrites for the browser floor or of style sheets (`oklch()` rounded); terser with a CSS minifier (two dependencies); zstd copies (brotli is smaller); budgets on minified files (a third looser); committing `vendor/` or generated files, `sw.js`'s version too (stamped where served); a version script it imports (HTTP-cached update checks); runtimes from a CDN; `transformers.min.js` (its own ORT); a monorepo (`packages/core`, `packages/web`: a build step by another name); `LICENSE` only in `app/` (forges look at the root) or twice; `pages/`, `config.js`, `public/` or `assets/` in `app/` (the service worker's scope; display only; all is served); one test runner (`node --test` drives no browser), `test/unit/` and `test/e2e/` (a capability's tests apart), or tests beside modules (`app/` holds what ships).
- **D10 · Custom elements with `@preact/signals-core`, `lit-html` and `marked`'s lexer, the only libraries loaded upfront.** *Why:* standard elements upgrade when their module loads, which *is* progressive discovery, and all of it is CSP-safe. *Rejected:* Preact with htm; VanJS; Alpine (needs `eval`); htmx (needs a server).
- **D627 · A long list draws only what is in view, through one directive of Jean's own, `virtual()`: a design's takes row, each group of Creations and the drawer's tasks** ([architecture.md](architecture.md#stack)). *Why:* a scrolled takes row kept every take it dealt, each deal costing more (325 ms at 500 takes, 8,219 elements; now 1,389); Creations drew every card (20,000 elements at 1,500, 65 ms a keystroke, 40 of them each card's heading worked out again, now once a day); the drawer drew each task with its menu, twice (150,128 elements at 3,000, 74 ms whenever the open task changed): performance everywhere (the owner, 26 October 2026). *Rejected:* `@lit-labs/virtualizer` (a dependency on Lit's elements, with no groups); `content-visibility: auto` alone, which Creations had (every card still built and drawn again); the list's padding for the rest (a scroller's parent grows to hold it); a helper for each list.
- **D66 · QuickJS and Playwright:** `quickjs-emscripten-core` with only the release-sync variant (~600 KB with its wasm), loaded on demand; `@playwright/test`, a dev dependency, run by `npm run e2e` in Chromium and WebKit against the fake brain, apart from `npm run check`. *Rejected:* the `quickjs-emscripten` package with every variant; browsers inside `npm run check`.
- **D701 · Jean's own Node, what CI, the container images and Jean's server run, is the newest LTS, named once in `.nvmrc`; `engines` in `package.json` stays the oldest the scripts run on, for `Intl`'s features.** Every `node:<major>` image names the same (`test/lts.test.js`), every CI job sets Node up from the file over its image's own, and `npm run lts`, in CI on every push and each Monday, fails once Node's list of releases names a newer LTS; `-- --write` moves them all. *Why:* the images had fallen behind, the server's to 22, and Playwright's image brings its own. *Rejected:* `node:lts` and `lts/*` (the major moves under a build unseen, and no build can be repeated); Dependabot (no notion of LTS: it offers odd majors); images and deploys waiting on nodejs.org.
- **D724 · `npm run ci` replays GitHub's CI on the machine, reading the workflows themselves: every job in the image it names, side by side, and what publishes rehearsed, never pushed** ([architecture.md](architecture.md#deploying)). An action, an input or a condition `scripts/ci.js` can't replay fails `npm test` (`test/ci.test.js`), so a workflow change brings its replay. *Why:* the owner wants the whole CI, browser tests and images included, to pass here before a push, since a red run on main stops the deploy (D659); a list of jobs kept by hand drifts from the workflow it copies. *Rejected:* act (a runner image unlike GitHub's, kind unreachable from inside it, no way to rehearse a deploy without running it); CI calling scripts instead of actions (it loses the actions' caches and pins); a YAML library (one dependency more, for the subset the workflows write).
