# Decisions

Each entry is a decision as it stands: what was chosen, why, and what was rejected, so it isn't proposed again. Numbers are stable, because code, docs and commits cite them. When a later decision amended an earlier one, both live in one entry under all their numbers, so searching for any `Dnnn` lands on the current rule; git history keeps the original wording. A number heads one entry only: a decision that changed several heads the one it changed most, and the others name it. `test/decisions.test.js` checks that every number cited heads exactly one entry. D124 and D151 were never used.

An entry is 200 words at most, its heading and its Rejected list included, the numbers heading it apart; how it works goes into [architecture.md](architecture.md) or [product.md](product.md), once, and the entry links to it. A new decision takes the next number after the highest one here, checked again just before writing, since several sessions may add numbers at once. It goes into the section it belongs to; if it changes an existing entry, rewrite that entry to the new state and add the number to its heading. A budget is raised only by the owner, in a change of its own.

## Product

- **D1 · Reboot from scratch** rather than refactor DEVS. *Why:* no users or years of fixes to protect; cutting 95% is harder than writing ~5k clean lines. *Rejected:* pruning DEVS ([background.md](background.md)).
- **D2 · D395 · D566 · Jean's promise is “Create with any AI, keep everything”: everyone's AI, non-technical people first, at home, in a family and at work, one box, anything in, anything out, with any brain.** What sets it apart, in this order: simple, universal, confidential, ready for a family, open, shareable, each shown by what it makes (D5; [product.md](product.md#who-and-what)). The promise names what ChatGPT can't: any brain, its rivals' included, and history, files and memory kept on the device, brought in and taken anywhere; “in a browser tab” says how. Files in, files out, searched most, leads the examples. *Why:* a files-first pitch hid most of Jean (17 October 2026); “Everyone's AI, in a browser tab” was as true of ChatGPT, while searches in five languages ask how to leave ChatGPT, keep its history and find every AI in one place (24 October). *Rejected:* files in, things out as the identity; developers first; generic chat; “private” (Proton and DuckDuckGo own it); a mode for code; “Everyone's AI, in a browser tab”; “AI operating system” and “AI workloads” (builders' words); “own your creations” (OpenAI's terms give users their output; it reads as copyright); “all AI” (aggregators); a full stop mid-promise (the title stops there).
- **D473 · Jean keeps what it makes, tables first, then documents and decks (D485, D491).** A table Jean builds from a request and keeps up to date comes first ("keep track of my job applications": typed columns, a board by status, a routine); then documents kept and edited beside the conversation, Jean's changes marked with Keep and Undo; decks stay Marp documents with their slides beside them, and .pptx out. Each is born in a task and found as a capability is ([roadmap.md](roadmap.md)). *Why:* a database is Notion's strongest feature and its hardest to learn, and a brain takes that away; boards, calendars, forms and apps' data are views of a table; documents are expected everywhere, and decks are PowerPoint's and Gamma's. *Rejected:* every editor in Jean's own code (thousands of lines each); an office suite inside (Univer, OnlyOffice, BlockSuite: megabytes, a build, formats of their own); others or brains building the three (Japanese input, Arabic and screen readers left to chance, a format each); a tree of pages to organise (the one box, D5); decks edited as PowerPoint does.
- **D5 · Progressive discovery, like a game:** Jean starts as one box, and a capability's code loads when it is discovered. *Why:* the simple first screen and the small first load are one decision. *Rejected:* a feature-complete UI from day one.
- **D433 · A capability found turns off, and on again, from its page in Settings › Capabilities** ([product.md](product.md#progressive-discovery), [architecture.md](architecture.md#stack)). Turned off, it stays found but dark, denied as policy denies one (never offered, loaded or shown) until its page turns it on, at once. A module can't be unloaded, so a page that has it opens afresh where it was once nothing is under way, in every tab. Things Jean made is always there, and a lock and the brain on this device are undone where they are set, so these don't turn off; nor does anything in a private window, which opening afresh would empty. *Why:* something found by chance, or no longer wanted, shouldn't stay for good. *Rejected:* unloading in place (its timers, listeners and elements go on: routines would still run); forgetting it was found (offered again); a switch in each capability's own section (thirty places); a second way for private windows (two behaviours); a note to conversations that declared its tools (they are gone, as a removed connection's are).
- **D6 · Discovery is triggered by events, reflexes and the brain's `offer()`, never by keywords.** *Why:* it works in every language. *Rejected:* regex or keyword intent rules.
- **D7 · D224 · D225 · One agent loop, plus helpers, meetings of the projects' assistants, and methods as ready-made skills.** Delegation is one tool (D98); a meeting has the other projects' assistants speak, without tools, and asks first, a project's memory being its own ([architecture.md](architecture.md#meetings)); six skills each pick a method ([product.md](product.md#instructions-and-memory)). *Why:* 2026's winners are single-loop agents; a project with a name is what others call an agent, so people ask their agents to meet; a method is steps, as a skill is. *Rejected:* DEVS's decomposer, standing teams with roles, persona libraries and agent patterns (greeter, operator, orchestrator, listener): the swarm; methodologies as modes of the loop; speakers with tools (acting stays the chair's); meetings among assistants, or endless rounds; a transcript per meeting; each speaker on its own project's brain (one answer mixing keys); a skill per method (a `<skills>` line each, tiles alike); a method field in `project.md` (instructions say it); developers' methods (test-, behaviour- and domain-driven design, XP, the V-model, RAD, Crystal, SAFe, Scrum for software), the first user writing no code (D2); TQM (a culture); Six Sigma beyond DMAIC; YOLO (Jean without a skill).
- **D27 · D242 · Data is bound to the origin, so the domain is settled before launch: jean.is, bought.** The desktop app's bundle id is its reverse, `is.jean.desktop`. *Why:* changing it means moving everyone's data. *Rejected:* launching on a temporary domain; jean.cx.
- **D38 · D50 · Jean is its own project, in its own repository,** moved out of DEVS with its history (`git subtree split`): nothing read, imported or copied from outside the repository, and no devs.new redirect. *Rejected:* Jean as DEVS's successor; a DEVS subfolder.
- **D3 · D39 · D139 · Open core: MIT, with the enterprise integrations in `ee/` under the Elastic License 2.0 and a key.** Reputation first, money second. Everything a person or a team uses is MIT; `ee/` holds only what an organisation's IT, security or compliance team asks for, never the person's own security, a standard Jean speaks or anything already open, and nothing moves from open to `ee/`. One module loads `ee/` with `import()`, only when policy holds a key, checked offline inside `ee/`; `ee/GRANT.md` makes every version MIT if no key can be bought for twelve consecutive months ([enterprise.md](enterprise.md#open-and-enterprise)). *Why:* small players get the whole product, big ones adopt it bottom-up and pay when their IT needs the integrations; Elastic-2.0 is short, on the SPDX list and known to legal teams; Ed25519 would need browsers above D40's floor. *Rejected:* BUSL on everything; AGPL with a commercial licence; FCL (not on the SPDX list); PolyForm Small Business; a custom licence; online key checks; keys that stop a running version; locked features shown in the open version.
- **D45 · D226 · D477 · One relay, not built, for what needs browsers to reach each other: replies to what was shared, requests between people's Jeans, and a person's phone driving their Jean (D227).** Hosted by the maintainer on a subdomain of Jean's domain, it runs only once something needs it, forwards end-to-end-encrypted traffic it can't read, and is self-hosted or off by policy (`urls.collaboration`); a link's `#fragment` carries the key. Replies to a shared document or table (comments, suggestions, form rows) come sealed to the sender to merge (D477). A colleague's request arrives as a card, read as data, answered by the recipient's own brain, keys and rules, reading only unless allowed (D101), approved before anything goes back; A2A is the likely format. Secrets never cross. *Why:* a person's agent is their project, on their device; working with colleagues means asking theirs, each keeping its keys and say; turns before live editing. *Rejected:* no public relay; a relay that can read content; a Jean server that runs agents; a request run with the sender's authority; one person's brain answering for another; working live together for now (until replies go back and forth weekly; D474 keeps it possible).

## Desktop

- **D155 · D160 · A desktop app, with Neutralino:** the files a deploy serves, embedded in Neutralino's binary and served on a fixed port of 127.0.0.1 to a window of the system's own web view, its native API on only for the calls Sync, Settings › Computer and This computer need; on the Mac, a launcher lets sites that refuse frames show in the browser pane ([architecture.md](architecture.md#desktop-app)). None of its names or identifiers come from `BRAND`, and none may change once in use. *Why:* the system's web view stays lean, and the window serves exactly what a deploy copies; the port is fixed since the origin holds the data. *Rejected:* Electron; Tauri; Neutralino's random port; its client library; a native bridge beyond what Sync, Settings › Computer and This computer ask; `os.execCommand`, or any program but the bridge and the list of addresses.
