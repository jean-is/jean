# Design something new

Shape something new with the people it is for: understand them first, then find the right problem, then try ideas cheaply before building anything.

## Choose the approach

1. When the room's instructions name one, use it.
2. Otherwise pick:
   - **The double diamond**, with design thinking's practices, by default: discover widely, define one problem, develop many ideas, deliver by testing and refining.
   - **DMADV**, when what the people it serves need can be measured (a waiting time, a delivery window, an error rate): their requirements as numbers, a design that meets them, and a pilot that checks it does.
3. When it isn't clear, ask with `ask_user`, recommending one.

## Steps

1. **Who it is for** and what the user wants to change, with what they already know: complaints, reviews, emails, survey answers, sales figures. Read what they share.
2. **Discover.** Propose how to learn from those people: an interview guide (open questions about what they do, try and feel, never "would you like…?"), what to watch them do, and a short survey when there are many of them. Sum up the answers the user brings back: what people do, need and struggle with, quoting them [n].
3. **Define.** Three to five insights; a persona or two drawn from real answers, with no invented details; the journey as a table (step, what they do, think and feel, what hurts); and the problem as one question: "How might we … for … so that …?"
4. **Develop.** At least ten ideas, some ordinary, some bold. Then the best three weighed in a table against what people need, what it costs and how hard it is, its corner empty and a ★ after the one you recommend.
5. **Deliver.** The cheapest prototype that can be tried: a sketch described in words, a leaflet, a script for a phone call, a form, or a page made on the computer and shown beside the conversation. A test with about five people: what to ask them to do, what to watch, and what would say it works. Then what to change, and test again.
6. **For DMADV,** the requirements as measures with targets, each choice of the design against them, and a pilot that measures them before the launch.

## Before you answer

Check that the problem comes from what people said or did, not from the solution the user had in mind; that each persona rests on real answers; and that the test could fail. Say plainly what is still an assumption.

## Offer

The interview guide and the summary of what was learned as Word, the ideas and the test results as Excel with `write`, and the prototype as a page to try in the browser.
