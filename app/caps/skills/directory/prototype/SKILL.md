# Make a prototype

A prototype shows an idea for an app, a form or a service: a few screens, each its own page, that a person clicks through as if it worked. Make it with `prototype`, read the check it returns, fix what it says, and only then hand it over. Don't ask first: decide what the request leaves open, and before you make it say in one line its pages in order and whether it is for a phone or a computer. A prototype is never a ```html page in your answer nor a file written with `write`: only `prototype` keeps it and checks it.

## Steps

1. Take what the request gives: who uses it, the path they follow from the first screen to the last, the facts (names, services, prices, dates), the files shared, and the language.
2. List the screens, three to eight, each with one job, in the order a person meets them: `index.html` first, the others in plain lowercase words (`time.html`, `confirm.html`). Then read `prototype/screens` (with `skill`) for the shape that fits: a phone app, the steps of a form, or a dashboard. Give `shape`: `phone` for an app in a column, `computer` for a dashboard or a wide form.
3. Every screen is reached from the first, the way forward before the ways elsewhere, and every control does something a person sees: a link or a button leads to another screen, or changes this one (opens a panel, switches a tab, picks a time, adds a line). Never a button that does nothing, never `href="#"`, and never `location.href` from a script, which Jean's frame can't follow: go to another screen with a link or a form. A choice a later screen shows travels in the address: a link such as `confirm.html?time=10:30`, or a form whose `action` is the next screen, its fields going along; `prototype.js` reads them with `URLSearchParams`. Nothing is kept anywhere else: no `localStorage`, no cookies.
4. It looks real and doesn't pretend: real words in the user's language, sample names and figures that fit the subject and agree with each other (a date on its real weekday, a sum that adds up, a choice shown later as it was made), never lorem ipsum, and one small line on the first screen saying it is a prototype with sample data. Where a real app would sign in or take a payment, show what the person would see with no field for a password, a card or a bank number: a "Continue as Sara" button, or the card written as words ("Visa ending 4242, sample") above a "Pay €29 (demo)" button that leads to the next screen. Where it would send something, say what the real app would do ("The teacher would get this message").
5. Read `site/look` (with `skill`) for colours and type: everything through the properties of `look.css`, which `prototype` writes from its `look`, never a colour of your own. One `style.css` for every screen.
6. Each screen has `<!doctype html>`, `<html lang dir>`, `<meta charset="utf-8">`, the viewport line, a `<title>` naming the screen, then the prototype (`Time · Maison Léa`), `look.css` then `style.css`, one `<main>` with one `<h1>`, and headings in order. A way back wherever a person would look for one. Links go to another screen, `<button type="button">` stays on this one. Every field has a `<label for>`; every picture an `alt`, or `alt=""` when it only decorates.
7. A script only in `prototype.js`, linked from each screen that needs it with `<script src="prototype.js" defer></script>`, never inside a page and never `onclick=""`. No `alert()`, `confirm()` or `prompt()`, which Jean's frame doesn't show: write the message on the screen. No framework, no library, nothing loaded from another site.
8. A phone first, a computer too: at 390 pixels nothing scrolls sideways or is cut, at 1280 nothing stretches thin. A phone app keeps a centred column; a dashboard uses the width. Sizes in `rem`, logical properties only (`margin-inline-start`, `text-align: start`), so a right-to-left language reads with nothing added.
9. Call `prototype` with its shape, then every file: `style.css` first, `prototype.js` if there is one, then the screens in the order of step 2. Its pane opens while you write the call, and the user watches each screen come. Its check presses every control from `index.html` on, sees each screen at 320 and 1280 pixels, and says which screens nobody reaches, which controls do nothing or leave by a script, and what a screen reader would miss. Fix with `prototype_change` for a few passages, or `prototype` again with its place for many. After three rounds without a clean report, make it smaller, not larger.
10. Hand it over in two short lines: the screens in order and what each shows; what is sample. The three requests your answer ends with are ideas to take it further, each in words the user could send (“Add a page to cancel a booking”). Don't end with a question.
11. To change it later, use `prototype_change` with the smallest passages, and keep every link and every screen's words right.

## Build it for real

When the user presses Build it, or asks you to build the prototype, you are given every file of it. Read the skill `app` (with `skill`) and make the app the prototype shows as it says, with what a prototype adds:

1. Each screen of the prototype is a view of the app, the first screen first, with the same words, the same order and the same ways between them. The choices that travelled in the address are kept by the app's script.
2. Its look is the prototype's, not the room's: copy the properties of `look.css` (`--paper`, `--ink`, `--accent`…) into the page's own style, by day and by night.
3. What the prototype only showed becomes real: its sample data is what the app starts with, and what a person enters shows wherever the prototype showed it. Where it said what a real app would do (signing in, a payment, a message sent), keep its words.
4. Say in two lines what works now and what still only shows.

The prototype stays as it was, beside the app.

## What a prototype isn't

A prototype shows; it keeps nothing, sends nothing and takes no money. Its screens never ask for a password, a card or an identity number, and never say something was sent, saved or paid as if it were. When the request needs pages for strangers to read and find, that is a site.
