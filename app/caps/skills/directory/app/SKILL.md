# Make an app

An app is one page that works in your answer: a fenced code block tagged `html`, which runs where it is, is kept as an app under Creations and opens beside the task. Decide what the request leaves open and keep going: a first version that works is worth more than a question. For a sum to try other numbers on, rows to add to or key figures with a chart, the page of parts that `visual` gives needs no code and is quicker: write an app when it has screens, things to remember or a behaviour of its own.

## Steps

1. Take what the request gives: who uses it, what they do in it again and again, what it must remember, the facts and files shared, and the language. Say in one line what it does and its screens, then write the page at once.
2. One page, whole, in one block: a `<title>`, the app's name in two or three words, by which it is kept; then its markup, one `<style>` and one `<script>`. Start at the `<title>`: the page is already given its `<html>`, with the user's language and direction. Nothing from elsewhere: no framework, no library, no font, no picture from the internet. A chart is ECharts, `import * as echarts from 'echarts'` in a `<script type="module">`; a drawing is SVG.
3. Its screens are views of that page: each a `<section>` with one heading, one shown at a time (`hidden` on the others), the first screen first. A `<button type="button">` changes the view; a link is only for another site, and opens a new tab. Showing a view moves the focus to its heading (`tabindex="-1"`), and the button of the view on show has `aria-current="page"`. A way back wherever a person would look for one.
4. It remembers. What a person enters or chooses is kept in `localStorage`, as JSON under one key, 256 KB at most, read as the page starts and written at each change: Jean keeps it for the app, so it is as it was left. What is kept shows wherever it belongs: a new entry in its list, the total added up again, a filter that filters, a count that counts. With nothing yet, a screen says what to do first; sample entries, said to be samples, only where the app would say nothing without them, and the person can take them away.
5. Every control does something a person sees. A form says what is missing beside its field, in words, and adds nothing until it is right; its `submit` is handled by the script (`event.preventDefault()`), since it goes nowhere else. What is taken away can be brought back, with an Undo beside the words that say it went. No `alert()`, `confirm()` or `prompt()`, which are never shown: write the message on the page, in a `role="status"` region.
6. Its look is the room's, by day and by night: colours and type only through `--color-text-primary`, `--color-text-secondary`, `--color-border-primary`, `--color-background-secondary`, `--color-ring-primary` (the accent), `--color-background-inverse` with `--color-text-inverse` on it (the main button), `--color-text-danger`, `--font-sans` and `--border-radius-lg`. No background on the page itself, and never a colour of your own. Buttons and fields already have their look: add only what the app needs.
7. A phone first: at 320 pixels nothing scrolls sideways or is cut; on a wide pane, lines keep a readable width. Sizes in `rem`, logical properties only (`margin-inline-start`, `text-align: start`), so a right-to-left language reads with nothing added. To fill its pane, as a board or a drawing does, make `body` `100vh` tall.
8. Every word is in the user's language; numbers, sums, dates and plurals through `Intl`. Every field has a `<label for>`, every button its words or an `aria-label`; nothing is told by colour alone; what a drag does, a button does too.
9. Beyond itself, a page may read a file the person picks (`<input type="file">`), save one it makes (`<a download>` on a `blob:` or `data:` address), and, on a click, ask you with `sendPrompt(text)`, which sends the text as the user's next message.
10. It is played before your answer ends, out of sight: every button pressed once, every field changed, nothing kept. So nothing throws on a button pressed with its fields empty. Told what broke, write the page again whole, fixed, after a sentence saying what you fixed.
11. Hand it over in two lines: what it does and what it remembers; what it only shows, if anything. The three requests your answer ends with take it further, each in words the user could send. Don't end with a question.
12. To change it later, write the whole page again with only what was asked changed, under the same `<title>` and the same key, so that what it kept is still there: read what is kept as it is, and give what is new a value to start with.

## What an app can't do

An app works on this device, for the person in front of it. It has no server: no accounts, no sign-in, no payment, nothing sent to someone else, nothing shared between people. Where a real app would do one of these, say what it would do in a line on the page, and never draw a field for a password, a card or an identity number. Pages for strangers to read and find are a site; screens that show an idea before it works, a prototype; something to play, a game.
