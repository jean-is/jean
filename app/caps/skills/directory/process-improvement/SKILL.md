# Improve a process

Make a way of working faster, simpler or cheaper, starting from how it really runs today, and keep the gain.

## Choose the approach

1. When the room's instructions name one, use it.
2. Otherwise pick:
   - **Small steps (Kaizen)**, by default: take the waste out a step at a time, with the people who do the work, each change tried and kept if it works.
   - **The bottleneck first (Theory of Constraints)**, when work piles up at one step while the others wait: find it, get the most out of it, make the other steps follow its pace, only then add capacity there, and look for the next one.
   - **A redesign**, when the gain needed is large (half the time or the cost) or the process no longer fits: start from what the people it serves need, not from today's steps.
3. When it isn't clear, ask with `ask_user`, recommending one.

## Steps

1. **What good looks like:** where the process starts and ends, who it serves, what they need, and the measure to improve (time from start to end, cost, errors, complaints) with today's number. Ask only what you can't find, all at once.
2. **The process as it is,** from what the user describes or shares (procedures, emails, exports): a table with a row per step, who does it, how long the work takes, how long it waits before it, and what goes wrong there. Mark the handoffs and the approvals. Never fill a gap with a guess: write "not known" and say how to find out (time a few cases, ask the people who do it).
3. **Where the time goes.** Work out, on the computer when there are numbers, the time spent working against the time from start to end, and draw it in an ```echarts block, an ECharts option in strict JSON: a horizontal bar chart, a bar per step, working and waiting as two series with the same `"stack"`. Name the bottleneck: the step with the longest queue or the least capacity.
4. **The waste,** in office terms, each where it happens: waiting, the same data typed twice, searching, approvals that add nothing, mistakes and rework, unclear handoffs, steps nobody needs, work done before anyone asks for it, and the ideas of the people doing the work left unused.
5. **Improvements:** for the bottleneck and each waste, at least three ideas weighed in a table: what changes, the gain expected, the cost and effort, the risk. Leave its corner empty and put a ★ after the one you recommend. Prefer simple changes the team can make themselves: a checklist, a template, one form instead of two, an approval only above an amount.
6. **The process as it will be,** as a new table of steps, with its time and cost against today's.
7. **Try it first,** on a few cases or for a week: what to measure, and the result that would say it worked. Then make it the standard: the steps written down, a checklist or a template, who owns the process, and the measure looked at each week or month.
8. When some of the steps are work Jean can do (gathering, totalling, filling in a document), suggest saving them as a flow.

## Before you answer

Check that today's numbers are facts or marked as estimates, the gain is a number, each change has an owner and a date, and nothing blames a person. Name the risks of the change, and who it affects, in a `> [!WARNING]` note.

## Offer

The process map and the plan as Excel with `write` (today's steps, the new ones, the actions with owner, date and measure), the new procedure as Word, and a routine that looks at the measure again on a date.
