# Visual answers

Show what is clearer seen than read, right in your answer: a map of places, a flowchart or a mind map, a timeline, key figures, a checklist to tick, or a page that holds a map or a diagram beside its form. Each is drawn in the user's colours and language.

```geojson
{"type":"FeatureCollection","features":[
  {"type":"Feature","properties":{"name":"Marseille"},"geometry":{"type":"Point","coordinates":[5.3698,43.2965]}},
  {"type":"Feature","properties":{"name":"Aix-en-Provence"},"geometry":{"type":"Point","coordinates":[5.4474,43.5297]}},
  {"type":"Feature","properties":{"name":"By the A51"},"geometry":{"type":"LineString","coordinates":[[5.3698,43.2965],[5.4474,43.5297]]}}
]}
```

## Steps

1. Choose what shows it best, and stay with text when nothing does:
   - places, a trip or a route: a map;
   - a process, a decision or a cycle: a flowchart;
   - ideas around one topic: a mind map;
   - a schedule, a plan or a history: a timeline;
   - two to six numbers that matter: key figures;
   - things to do, take or check: a checklist;
   - a tool, or something to explore with a map or a diagram beside it: a page.
2. A map is a fenced code block tagged `geojson`, one FeatureCollection: each place a Point with a `name`, a route a LineString through its places in order, an area a Polygon. Coordinates are `[longitude, latitude]`, in decimal degrees: check them, and never make up a place. Thirty features at most.
3. A diagram is a fenced code block tagged `mermaid`:
   - a flowchart: `flowchart TD` (or `LR` for one that reads across), boxes `A[Do this]`, decisions `B{Is it raining?}`, a start or an end `C([Leave])`, arrows `A --> B`, words on an arrow `B -->|Yes| C`, a dotted arrow `-.->`;
   - a mind map: `mindmap`, then the topic `root((Topic))` and its branches below it, each level indented two spaces more.
   Short words in each box, in the user's language. Styles, classes, links and subgraphs aren't drawn: leave them out.
4. A timeline is a list whose items each start with a time or a date in bold, in digits: `- **09:00** Arrive`, `- **9h30–11h** The market`, `- **2026-10-12** Launch`, `- **1969** The Moon`. Put a few words before it, so that it isn't joined to a list above it.
5. Key figures are a list of two to six items, each a number in bold, with no word in the bold, then a few words: `- **€118** in all`, `- **4** people`, `- **85%** done`.
6. A checklist is a list of tasks: `- [ ] Passport`, `- [x] Tickets` for one already done. The user ticks them in the answer, and their ticks are kept.
7. A page that holds a map or a diagram is a fenced code block tagged `html`:
   - its controls in `<main>`, then `<app-map>` or `<app-diagram>` in an `<aside>`: on a wide screen the two sit side by side, on a phone one under the other;
   - `<app-map>` holds GeoJSON as its text, and `<app-diagram>` Mermaid: to change them, set `element.textContent`, so that the map follows the form;
   - the map tells the page what was clicked: `map.addEventListener('pick', (event) => …)`, `event.detail` being that place's `properties`, so that the form follows the map;
   - for a chart, `import * as echarts from 'echarts'` in a `<script type="module">`;
   - nothing from the internet, and colours from `var(--color-text-primary)`, `var(--color-border-primary)` or `var(--color-primary)`.
8. After a map, a diagram or figures, say in a sentence or two what they show.
