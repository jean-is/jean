# Goals and strategy

Turn what someone wants to achieve into goals that can be measured, the actions that reach them, and a way to check progress.

## Choose the approach

1. When the room's instructions name one, use it.
2. Otherwise pick:
   - **Objectives and key results (OKR)**, by default, for a quarter or a year: a few objectives, each with results that say in numbers whether it was reached.
   - **Hoshin Kanri**, for an organisation over a year or more: a few breakthrough objectives drawn from the vision, the year's priorities that serve them, each team's part, and a review each month. Drafts go to the teams and come back with their say before they are fixed.
   - **A strategy plan** (analysis, objectives, strategies, tactics, control), for a business, a product or a campaign whose reasoning needs to be written down.
3. When it isn't clear, ask with `ask_user`, recommending one.

## Steps

1. **The frame:** who the goals are for, the period, what matters most now, what was tried before, and the numbers known today. Ask only what is missing, all at once.
2. **The situation,** for a strategy plan, from what the user shares and, when they ask, the web, citing each source [n]: the customers, the competitors, the strengths, weaknesses, opportunities and threats, as a table.
3. **Three to five objectives,** each a sentence that says what will be better and why it matters: an outcome, not a task.
4. **Two to four key results for each:** a measure, today's number and the target, with a date ("from 12% to 20% of quotes accepted by 30 June"). Results, not activities: "publish ten posts" is an action. When there is no number today, the first result is to measure it.
5. **Actions,** or tactics: what will be done to move each result, by whom, by when and with what budget. Say what will not be done, so the goals stay few.
6. **For Hoshin Kanri,** a table that links each objective to the year's priorities, their measures and their owners, which objective each team serves, and what to ask the teams before the plan is fixed.
7. **Progress,** once there are numbers, drawn in an ```echarts block, an ECharts option in strict JSON: a horizontal bar per key result, its share of the target.
8. **Check-ins:** when (each week or month) and what is looked at; at the end, a grade for each key result from 0 to 1, where 0.7 is good for an ambitious goal, and what was learned.

## Before you answer

Check that every key result has a number, a starting point and a date, the objectives are few, each has an owner, and no task is dressed up as a result.

## Offer

The plan as Word, the tracker as Excel with `write` (objective, key result, start, target, now, owner, date), and the check-ins as a routine.
