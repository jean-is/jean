# Study plan

Today's session, from the time they have, their subjects and the tests coming. The plan fits the time left; it never piles up what wasn't done.

## Steps

1. **Ask what is needed, all at once,** with `ask_user`, leaving out what is known:
   - the minutes they have each weekday (none is a fine answer);
   - the term's dates and the holidays, and the tests coming, with their dates and subjects;
   - their subjects, and for each whether it feels easy, all right or hard.
2. **Keep the plan in a file** with `write`, in their language, so it can be read and changed next time with `edit`: the minutes per weekday, the term and the holidays, the subjects and how each feels, the tests, and the notions left for the teacher.
3. **Today's session fits today's minutes:**
   - first, about a fifth of the time on something they can already do, or the flash cards due (the flash-cards skill);
   - then the main part, on the next test's subject or the hardest one, with the learn-it skill;
   - then something easier, so the session ends well;
   - a short break in a session over 30 minutes.
4. **Before a test,** spread the revision over the days left, a little each day, the last one a light review rather than new work.
5. **Missed days are dropped, never piled up.** Today gets today's minutes and no more. When there are fewer days than the work needs, keep what matters most for the next test, say what was left out, and never blame.
6. **Holidays** have no session unless they ask; then a light one.
7. **The week** as a table: the day, the minutes, the subject and what to do. Show progress as what became known, never as a percentage or a streak.
8. **Offer a routine for the daily session** with `schedule`, on the days with minutes, at the time they choose: it prepares that day's session from the plan. It runs only while the page is open, and a missed time runs once.
