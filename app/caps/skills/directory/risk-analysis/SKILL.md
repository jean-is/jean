# What could go wrong

Find the risks before they happen, rank them, and plan what to do about the ones that matter.

## Choose the approach

1. When the room's instructions name one, use it.
2. Otherwise pick:
   - **A pre-mortem**, for a plan or a project, quickly: imagine it is over and it failed, then list every reason why.
   - **FMEA**, for a process, a product or a service with steps: for each step, how it could fail, what that would do, why it would happen, and how it would be noticed.
   - **A risk register**, for a project that runs for months: both, kept up to date.
3. When it isn't clear, ask with `ask_user`, recommending one.

## Steps

1. **What is looked at:** its steps or parts, its date, who it affects and what a failure would cost. Ask only what is missing, all at once.
2. **What could go wrong,** step by step or part by part, from the user's documents, from what went wrong before (incidents, complaints, earlier tasks) and from what usually goes wrong with this kind of thing. For each: its effect on people, money, time or reputation, and its cause.
3. **Rate each from 1 to 10:** how bad the effect would be (severity), how likely it is (occurrence), and how hard it would be to notice in time (detection, 10 for unnoticed). Say what each rating rests on. Rank anything that could hurt someone or break the law first, then by the product of the three, the risk priority number, computed exactly.
4. **Draw the ranking** in an ```echarts block: a `heatmap` with occurrence across and severity up, each cell the number of risks in it, with a `visualMap`; or, for a short list, a horizontal bar chart of the priority numbers, highest first. Name the risks in a table, highest first.
5. **Actions** for the risks that matter (a severity of 9 or 10, then the highest priority numbers): what makes each less likely, less harmful or noticed sooner, with an owner, a date and the ratings expected after it. For each, a fallback too: what to do if it happens anyway.
6. **What is accepted** as it is, and why.

## Before you answer

Check that each rating has a reason, the most severe risks have an action whatever their priority number, every action has an owner and a date, and no action is just "be careful". Put the worst risk in a `> [!WARNING]` note.

## Offer

The risk register as Excel with `write` (step, what could go wrong, effect, cause, severity, occurrence, detection, priority number, action, owner, date, ratings after), and the dates the actions are due as calendar events (an `.ics` file) or a routine.
