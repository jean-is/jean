# Project plan

Turn a goal and a deadline into tasks, dates and who does what, and say what can't slip.

## Choose the approach

1. When the room's instructions name one, use it.
2. Otherwise pick:
   - **Dates worked out from the tasks (the critical path)**, when the work is known and there is a deadline: an event, a move, a renovation.
   - **With ranges (PERT)**, when durations are uncertain: each task as its best case, its most likely and its worst.
   - **Phases with a sign-off**, when each phase must be approved before the next one starts (a building, a file for an authority): the phases, what each delivers, who approves it.
   - **Short cycles**, when the work can't be planned upfront (a website, a campaign): a week or two each, a board of tasks (to do, doing, done, with few in progress at once), and at the end of each cycle a look at what was done and what to change.
3. When it isn't clear, ask with `ask_user`, recommending one.

## Steps

1. **The frame:** the goal, the deadline, the budget, who takes part, and what is fixed already (a venue, a date, a supplier). Ask only what is missing, all at once.
2. **The tasks,** each one a person can own, from a day to two weeks, grouped in phases. For each: who, how long, and what it waits for.
3. **The dates,** worked out on the computer: each task's earliest start and end from what it waits for, the critical path (the chain of tasks with no slack, which sets the end date) and every other task's slack. With ranges, take (best + 4 × most likely + worst) / 6 for each task and say how likely the deadline is. Count working days in the user's country, and say which holidays you assumed.
4. **When the end comes after the deadline,** say by how much, and weigh the options in a table, its corner empty and a ★ after the one you recommend: more people on the critical path, tasks done side by side, less in scope, a later date.
5. **Milestones:** the few dates that matter (decisions, deliveries, the day itself).
6. **A Gantt chart** in an ```echarts block: the tasks on a category `yAxis` with `"inverse": true`, so the first is at the top; days from the start on a value `xAxis`; two `bar` series with the same `"stack"`, the first the days before the task starts, with `"itemStyle": { "opacity": 0 }` and `"tooltip": { "show": false }`, the second its duration, named in the user's language. Mark the tasks on the critical path with a ★ in their names.
7. **Risks and assumptions** to the plan, in a `> [!WARNING]` note.
8. **Following it:** a short status each week (done, late, next, blocked), and at the end a review: what went well, what didn't, and what to do differently next time.

## Before you answer

Check that every task has an owner and a duration, nothing waits on itself, the critical path is named, dates fall on working days, and the plan meets the deadline or says plainly that it doesn't.

## Offer

The plan as Excel with `write` (phase, task, owner, start, end, waits for, slack, status), the milestones as calendar events (an `.ics` file), and the weekly status as a routine.
